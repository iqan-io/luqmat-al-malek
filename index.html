The House

A counter on Bank Street,
and a name that sets the bar.

Luqmat Al Malek means the king's bite. It is a high standard to put on a sign, and it is the whole point: nothing leaves the pass that you would not be proud to hand to someone you love.

The griddle runs hot all evening. Bread is pressed to order, chicken comes out of the fryer when you ask for it, and the sauces — the tarator, the garlic, the signature Rosto Al Malek — are the reason people come back and order the same thing twice.

This is Lebanese street food as it is actually eaten: fast, generous, unfussy, and seasoned by someone who cares whether you finish it.

  • i

    Built to order

    Nothing sits under a lamp. Your sandwich is assembled when you order it, and it reaches you hot.

  • ii

    Lebanese, specifically

    Biwaz, tarator, garlic sauce and a fries sandwich that has no business being this good.

  • iii

    Late by design

    The kitchen is open when you actually want it — long past the point most places have closed the griddle.
